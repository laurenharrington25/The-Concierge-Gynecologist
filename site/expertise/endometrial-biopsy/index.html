Endometrial biopsy

An outpatient procedure to sample the lining of the uterus.

What an endometrial biopsy is

An endometrial biopsy takes a small sample of the endometrium, the lining of the uterus, so it can be examined under a microscope. It is a quick outpatient procedure and one of the most important tools for understanding abnormal bleeding.

Why it may be recommended

  • Bleeding after menopause
  • Heavy, prolonged or irregular bleeding, particularly after age 45 or with other risk factors
  • To check for thickening of the uterine lining (endometrial hyperplasia)
  • To monitor the lining in certain situations, such as some hormone treatments or hereditary cancer risks

What to expect

You lie on the exam table as you would for a pelvic exam. After a speculum is placed and the cervix is cleaned, a thin, flexible tube is passed through the cervix into the uterus, and a small sample of the lining is collected. The sampling itself usually takes less than a minute.

Many people feel cramping during the sampling, similar to strong period cramps. Dr. Harrington prioritizes your comfort and talks with you beforehand about options for pain medication, so you can plan ahead.

Afterward

Mild cramping and light spotting for a day or two are common. Most people return to their usual activities the same day. Results usually take several days, and Dr. Harrington will review them with you and discuss any next steps.

Contact the office if you have heavy bleeding, fever or severe or worsening pain after the procedure.

Common questions

Does an abnormal bleeding evaluation always need a biopsy?

Not always. Whether a biopsy is recommended depends on your age, your bleeding pattern, your risk factors and sometimes ultrasound findings.

Can I take something for pain beforehand?

Often, yes. Dr. Harrington will discuss pain control options with you before the procedure so you know what to take and when.
