The practice

How visits work

Do you take insurance?

Dr. Harrington does not accept insurance for appointments. This allows her to spend as much time needed to get to know you and create a comprehensive plan to address your concerns and symptoms. Your insurance can still be used for services outside of visits with Dr. Harrington: laboratory tests, imaging, and prescriptions (depending on your individual plan).

Where is your office?

Dr. Harrington currently partners directly with concierge primary care practices to see patients on-site at their practices. This allows for coordinated and collaborative wrap-around care for patients, and seamless communication between providers.

Subscribe to the newsletter to be the first to know about new partnerships and practice locations. If you’re interested in seeing Dr. Harrington, and your concierge primary care physician is not a current partner, please submit an inquiry to Dr. Harrington’s team.

How do I become a patient?

Reach out through the Get in Touch button, and the team will schedule an introductory conversation to see if the practice is the right fit.

Care and treatment

What is treated, and how

What is your philosophy on hormone therapy medications?

Dr. Harrington prescribes FDA-approved medications and treatments whenever one exists for your condition. Together, you and Dr. Harrington may consider compounded or non-FDA-approved options when no approved alternative is available.

Do you only treat perimenopause and menopause, or do you see patients for general gynecologic care too?

While hormone therapy, perimenopause, menopause, sexual function and cancer survivorship are a particular focus, Dr. Harrington also treats the full spectrum of gynecologic conditions, including: contraception, abnormal and postmenopausal bleeding, vulvar conditions, pelvic pain, abnormal pap smears. A first visit is the best way to find out where to start.

Do I need to already be in perimenopause or menopause to become a patient?

No. Many patients come in earlier, when symptoms are just beginning and hard to explain, or simply because they want a specialist relationship in place before they need it.

Alongside your other care

How this fits with the rest

I already have a primary care doctor or gynecologist — do I need to leave them?

Not at all. Many patients keep their existing primary care physician and see me specifically for specialized hormone and gynecologic care. I’m glad to coordinate with your other physicians when it’s helpful.

How is this different from online hormone therapy platforms?

Those platforms can be a reasonable starting point, but they typically don’t include an in-person exam, a full personal and family history, or ongoing access to the same physician. Every plan here is built around your specific history and stays with you as your needs change.
