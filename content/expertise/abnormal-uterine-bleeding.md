## What counts as abnormal bleeding

Abnormal uterine bleeding is bleeding from the uterus that is heavier, longer, more frequent or less predictable than expected, or that happens when it should not. Examples include:

- Very heavy periods, or periods that last longer than seven days
- Bleeding between periods or after sex
- Cycles that become much shorter, longer or unpredictable
- Any bleeding after menopause

Abnormal bleeding is common, especially in perimenopause, and it is not something you have to simply put up with. Heavy bleeding can cause fatigue and anemia and get in the way of daily life. Bleeding after menopause should always be evaluated.

## Common causes

- Hormonal changes, particularly in perimenopause
- Fibroids or polyps in the uterus
- Thickening of the uterine lining (endometrial hyperplasia)
- Thyroid conditions and other hormonal disorders
- Side effects of medications or contraception
- Less commonly, precancerous changes or cancer of the uterine lining

## How Dr. Harrington evaluates bleeding

Evaluation starts with a detailed history of your bleeding pattern and overall health, followed by an examination. Depending on your age, symptoms and risk factors, it may also include:

- Blood tests, for example to check for anemia or thyroid problems
- A pelvic ultrasound
- An [endometrial biopsy](/expertise/endometrial-biopsy/) to sample the lining of the uterus
- A [hysteroscopy](/expertise/hysteroscopy/) to look directly inside the uterus and treat polyps or other findings

Dr. Harrington performs these procedures herself and prioritizes your comfort, with appropriate pain medication.

## Treatment options

Treatment depends on the cause and on your goals, including whether you need contraception. Options may include hormonal treatments such as the hormonal IUD, non-hormonal medications that reduce heavy flow, treatment of polyps or other findings, and correction of anemia.

## When to seek urgent care

Very heavy bleeding, such as soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours, or bleeding with dizziness or fainting, needs urgent medical attention.

## Common questions

### Is irregular bleeding normal in perimenopause?

Changes in your cycle are common in perimenopause, but some patterns still deserve evaluation, such as very heavy bleeding, bleeding between periods or bleeding after sex. When in doubt, it is worth checking.

### I'm past menopause and had some spotting. Should I be seen?

Yes. Any bleeding after menopause should be evaluated, even if it is light or happens only once.
