## What a hysteroscopy is

A hysteroscopy uses a thin, lighted camera, called a hysteroscope, passed through the cervix to look directly inside the uterus. There are no incisions. It allows the inside of the uterus to be seen clearly and, in many cases, treated during the same procedure.

A hysteroscopy is often combined with a D&C (dilation and curettage), which gently removes tissue from the lining of the uterus for examination.

## Why it may be recommended

- Abnormal or heavy bleeding, or bleeding after menopause
- To find and remove polyps or certain fibroids inside the uterus
- When an ultrasound or biopsy shows something that needs a closer look
- To locate or remove an IUD that cannot be removed in the usual way

## What to expect

Depending on its purpose, a hysteroscopy may be done in the office or in an outpatient surgical setting. Dr. Harrington will explain which is right for you, how to prepare and what pain control or anesthesia will be used. She prioritizes your comfort and offers appropriate pain medication.

During the procedure, the hysteroscope is gently passed through the cervix and fluid is used to expand the uterus so it can be seen clearly. Any polyps or tissue that need removal are taken out with small instruments passed through the hysteroscope.

## Recovery

Most people go home the same day and return to normal activities within a day or two. Cramping and light bleeding for a few days are common. Results from any tissue removed usually take several days, and Dr. Harrington will review them with you.

Contact the office if you have heavy bleeding, fever or severe or worsening pain afterward.

## Common questions

### Is a hysteroscopy surgery?

It is a minimally invasive procedure with no incisions. Some hysteroscopies are done in the office, and others in an outpatient surgical setting, depending on what needs to be done.

### How is it different from an endometrial biopsy?

An [endometrial biopsy](/expertise/endometrial-biopsy/) samples the lining without seeing inside the uterus. A hysteroscopy lets the inside of the uterus be seen directly, so specific areas, such as polyps, can be targeted and removed.
