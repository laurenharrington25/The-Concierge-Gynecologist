## About vulvar conditions

The vulva is the external genital area. Like skin elsewhere on the body, it can be affected by a range of conditions, from irritation and infection to chronic skin diseases and nerve-related pain. Symptoms such as itching, burning, rawness or pain can be uncomfortable, affect sex and daily life, and are often misdiagnosed as a recurring yeast infection.

These conditions are common and treatable, but getting the right diagnosis matters. Many vulvar conditions look alike, and treatments that help one can make another worse.

## Conditions Dr. Harrington treats

- Chronic itching and irritation
- Lichen sclerosus and lichen planus
- Contact dermatitis from soaps, products or medications
- Recurrent yeast or other infections
- Vulvar pain without a clear skin cause (vulvodynia)
- Vulvar and vaginal changes of menopause

## How Dr. Harrington evaluates vulvar concerns

Evaluation begins with a detailed history, including symptoms, products you use and past treatments, followed by a careful examination. Testing may include samples to check for infection. When a skin condition needs to be confirmed, a small, quick vulvar biopsy can be done in the office with local numbing medication.

## Treatment

Treatment depends on the diagnosis and may include:

- Gentle skin care and removing irritating products
- Prescription creams or ointments, such as topical steroids for certain skin conditions
- Treatment of infection
- Vaginal hormone therapy for menopause-related changes
- Pelvic floor physical therapy and nerve-directed medications for vulvar pain

Some conditions, such as lichen sclerosus, benefit from ongoing follow-up, because long-term treatment keeps symptoms controlled and allows the skin to be monitored over time.

## Common questions

### I keep getting "yeast infections." Could it be something else?

Yes. Recurring itching or burning is often caused by something other than yeast, such as a skin condition or irritation from products. If treatments have not worked, a careful examination and testing are worthwhile.

### Is a vulvar biopsy painful?

The area is numbed with local anesthetic first, so most people feel only a brief pinch from the injection. The biopsy itself takes a few minutes.
