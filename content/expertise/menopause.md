## What menopause is

Menopause is the point at which menstrual periods stop permanently, confirmed after twelve consecutive months without a period. In the United States it happens on average around age 51. It can also occur earlier, after surgery that removes the ovaries or as a result of cancer treatment. The years that follow are called postmenopause.

The drop in estrogen at menopause affects far more than the reproductive system. Estrogen plays a role in temperature regulation, sleep, mood, the brain, the bones, the heart, the skin and the tissues of the vagina and bladder. That is why symptoms can be so varied, and why good menopause care looks at the whole picture.

## Common symptoms

- Hot flashes and night sweats
- Disrupted sleep
- Mood changes, anxiety and brain fog
- Vaginal dryness, irritation and pain with sex
- Urinary urgency, frequency or recurrent urinary tract infections
- Joint aches and changes in skin and hair
- Over the longer term, bone loss and changes in cardiovascular risk

## How Dr. Harrington approaches menopause

Dr. Harrington is a Menopause Society Certified Practitioner (MSCP). Every plan starts with a careful review of your symptoms, your personal and family medical history and your goals, so that treatment fits you rather than a template.

Options may include:

- **Hormone therapy.** For many healthy women within ten years of menopause, hormone therapy is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats, and it also protects bone. The type, dose and route are chosen to fit your health history and preferences.
- **Non-hormonal medications.** Several effective prescription options exist for women who cannot or prefer not to use hormones.
- **Vaginal therapies.** Low-dose vaginal estrogen and other treatments for dryness, discomfort and urinary symptoms.
- **Long-term health.** Attention to bone density, cardiovascular risk and the other ways menopause affects health over time.

FDA-approved medications are used whenever one exists for your condition. Compounded or non-FDA-approved options may be considered together when no approved alternative is available.

## What to expect

Menopause care is a relationship, not a single prescription. Dr. Harrington follows up to see how treatment is working and adjusts it over time, coordinating with your concierge primary care physician.

## Common questions

### Is hormone therapy safe?

For many healthy women who start it before age 60 or within ten years of menopause, the benefits of hormone therapy outweigh the risks. Whether it is right for you depends on your own health history, which is exactly what your visit is for.

### What if I can't take hormones?

There are effective non-hormonal medications for hot flashes, sleep and mood, and non-hormonal treatments for vaginal symptoms. Many women who cannot use systemic hormones can still use low-dose vaginal therapies; this is assessed individually.
