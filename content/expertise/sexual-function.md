## Sexual health is health

Changes in sexual function are common and often go unaddressed, because they can be hard to bring up and many clinicians do not have the time or training to help. Low desire, pain with sex, difficulty with arousal or orgasm and changes after childbirth, menopause or cancer treatment are medical concerns that deserve thoughtful care.

Dr. Harrington previously served as Co-Director of the Women's Sexual Health Consultation Service at the University of Colorado and teaches on female sexual health. She brings that experience to a conversation that is unhurried, private and judgment-free.

## Concerns Dr. Harrington treats

- Low or changed sexual desire
- Pain with sex or with penetration
- Difficulty with arousal or lubrication
- Difficulty reaching orgasm
- Changes related to perimenopause and menopause
- Sexual concerns after cancer treatment
- Changes after pregnancy and childbirth

## How Dr. Harrington approaches sexual function

Sexual function is influenced by hormones, the health of the vulvar and vaginal tissues, pelvic floor muscles, medications, other medical conditions, mood, stress and relationships. A good evaluation considers all of these.

Care begins with a detailed conversation, followed by an examination when it is helpful and you are comfortable with it. Treatment is tailored to the cause and may include:

- Treatment of vaginal dryness and tissue changes, including vaginal hormone therapy
- Systemic hormone therapy when appropriate
- FDA-approved medications for low desire, when appropriate
- Referral to pelvic floor physical therapy
- Review of medications that can affect sexual function
- Referral to a sex therapist or counselor when that would help

## What to expect

You set the pace. Nothing about the visit is rushed, and examinations happen only with your consent. Many concerns improve meaningfully with the right combination of treatments, and follow-up visits allow the plan to be adjusted over time.

## Common questions

### Is it normal for desire to change over time?

Desire naturally varies across life stages and relationships. Change becomes a concern when it causes you distress. If it does, it is worth evaluating, because there are often treatable contributing factors.

### Is pain with sex something I just have to live with?

No. Pain with sex has many possible causes, including tissue changes, skin conditions and pelvic floor muscle tension, and most of them can be treated.
