## What it means to be a previvor

A previvor is someone who carries an inherited predisposition to cancer but has not had cancer. Many previvors learn about their risk through genetic testing, often after a family member's diagnosis. Common examples include BRCA1 and BRCA2 gene changes and Lynch syndrome, which raise the risk of certain gynecologic and breast cancers.

Learning that you carry an inherited risk can bring up difficult questions about screening, prevention, family planning and the timing of risk-reducing surgery. Previvor care is about giving you clear information, a long-term plan and a consistent physician to guide you through those decisions over time.

## How Dr. Harrington approaches previvor care

Care begins with your genetic results, your personal and family history, your stage of life and your goals. Dr. Harrington then helps you build a surveillance and risk-reduction plan that fits you. Depending on your specific risk, that may include:

- Gynecologic surveillance appropriate to your genetic risk
- A discussion of risk-reducing options, including medications and surgery, and their timing
- Counseling about how risk-reducing surgery affects hormones, menopause and quality of life
- Coordination with genetic counselors, breast specialists and gynecologic oncologists as needed

## Hormone health for previvors

Some risk-reducing surgeries, such as removal of the ovaries, cause immediate menopause. Planning ahead for that transition makes a meaningful difference. For many previvors who have not had cancer, hormone therapy after risk-reducing surgery may be an option to manage symptoms and protect long-term health; the right approach depends on your specific situation.

## What to expect

Previvor care is ongoing. Dr. Harrington provides continuity, so you are not re-explaining your history to a new clinician each year, and your plan can evolve as your life and the medical evidence change. Care is coordinated with your concierge primary care physician.

## Common questions

### I just got my genetic results. Where do I start?

Start with an unhurried conversation about what your results mean for you. From there, a personalized plan can be made at a pace that feels right, alongside genetic counseling if you have not already had it.

### Will I have to go through menopause early?

Not necessarily, and not without a plan. If risk-reducing surgery is recommended, its timing is discussed in the context of your risk and your goals, and options for managing the hormonal effects are part of that conversation.
