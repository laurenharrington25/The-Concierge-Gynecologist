## Care after cancer treatment

Finishing cancer treatment is a milestone, but the effects of treatment often continue. Chemotherapy, radiation, surgery and hormone-blocking medications can bring on early or abrupt menopause, change the vaginal and vulvar tissues, affect sexual function and raise questions about bone health and long-term wellbeing.

These concerns are common and treatable, yet they are often overlooked during cancer care, when the focus is rightly on the cancer itself. Dr. Harrington provides dedicated time and specialized expertise for the gynecologic and hormonal side of survivorship.

## Concerns Dr. Harrington helps with

- Menopause symptoms after treatment, including sudden menopause caused by surgery or chemotherapy
- Hot flashes and night sweats while taking hormone-blocking medications such as tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors
- Vaginal dryness, irritation and pain with sex
- Changes in desire, arousal and orgasm
- Bone health after early menopause or hormone-blocking therapy
- Routine gynecologic care and screening after cancer

## How Dr. Harrington approaches survivorship care

Every plan starts with your cancer history, your current treatment and what matters most to you. Some cancers are sensitive to hormones, so treatment choices are made carefully and, when appropriate, in coordination with your oncology team.

Depending on your history, options may include:

- Non-hormonal medications for hot flashes and sleep
- Non-hormonal vaginal moisturizers and lubricants, and other vaginal therapies when they are considered appropriate for your history
- Pelvic floor physical therapy
- Treatment for sexual concerns, including referral to a sex therapist when helpful
- Evaluation of bone health
- Hormone therapy, for survivors of cancers in which it may be a safe option

## What to expect

You will have time to talk through what has changed and what you would like to feel like again. Care is coordinated with your concierge primary care physician and, when helpful, your oncologist, so that everyone caring for you is working from the same plan.

## Common questions

### Can I use any hormone therapy after cancer?

It depends on the type of cancer, its hormone sensitivity and your current treatment. For some survivors hormone therapy is reasonable; for others, non-hormonal options are preferred. This is a decision made individually and, when appropriate, together with your oncology team.

### Is it worth bringing up vaginal dryness or pain with sex?

Yes. These are among the most common effects of cancer treatment and among the most treatable. There are effective options, including non-hormonal ones.
