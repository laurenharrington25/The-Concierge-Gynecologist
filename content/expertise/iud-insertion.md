## About IUDs

An intrauterine device (IUD) is a small, T-shaped device placed in the uterus. It is one of the most effective forms of reversible contraception and can last for several years. Hormonal IUDs can also make periods lighter, and they are sometimes used to treat heavy bleeding or as part of hormone therapy.

## Hormonal and copper IUDs

- **Hormonal IUDs** release a small amount of progestin. They often make periods lighter, and some people stop having periods altogether.
- **The copper IUD** contains no hormones and lasts the longest. Some people notice heavier or crampier periods with it.

Choosing between them is part of your [contraception](/expertise/contraception/) conversation.

## Pain control

Worry about pain is one of the most common reasons people hesitate to get an IUD, and that concern is valid. Dr. Harrington prioritizes comfort and offers advanced pain control options, discussed with you in advance so you can choose what feels right and know what to expect.

## What to expect at the visit

You lie on the exam table as you would for a pelvic exam. After a speculum is placed and the cervix is cleaned, the size and position of the uterus are checked and the IUD is placed through the cervix with a thin inserter. The insertion itself usually takes only a few minutes. The strings are trimmed so they sit just beyond the cervix.

Cramping during and after insertion is common. Spotting and cramping can continue on and off for the first few weeks.

## Removal

IUD removal is usually quick and is done in the office. It can be done at any time, whether you want to switch methods, try to become pregnant or have reached menopause.

## Common questions

### How long does an IUD last?

It depends on the type; IUDs are approved to last anywhere from several years to a decade or more. Dr. Harrington will tell you how long yours is effective.

### Can I have an IUD placed in perimenopause?

Yes. A hormonal IUD can provide contraception, reduce heavy bleeding and, in some cases, serve as part of hormone therapy during the menopause transition.
