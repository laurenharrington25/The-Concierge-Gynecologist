## After an abnormal pap smear

An abnormal pap smear or a positive HPV test is common, and it rarely means cancer. Most abnormal results reflect HPV, a very common virus that the body often clears on its own. Some cell changes, however, need a closer look or treatment so they do not progress. Colposcopy and LEEP are the two main tools for that.

## Colposcopy

A colposcopy is a closer examination of the cervix with a magnifying instrument called a colposcope, which stays outside the body.

During the visit, a speculum is placed as for a pap smear. A mild solution is applied to the cervix to highlight areas of abnormal cells, and the cervix is examined under magnification. If an area looks abnormal, a small biopsy is taken. Most people feel a brief pinch or cramp with the biopsy. The visit usually takes 10 to 20 minutes, and light spotting or discharge for a few days afterward is common.

## LEEP

A LEEP (loop electrosurgical excision procedure) removes an area of abnormal cells from the cervix using a thin wire loop. It both treats the abnormal cells and provides tissue to confirm the diagnosis.

LEEP is usually done with local numbing medication and takes only a few minutes. Afterward, cramping and discharge are common for a few weeks while the cervix heals, and you will be asked to avoid sex and tampons for a period of time. Dr. Harrington will explain the recovery in detail and review your results with you.

## Follow-up

After colposcopy or treatment, follow-up testing confirms that the cervix remains healthy. Dr. Harrington provides continuity, so the same physician follows your results over time.

## Common questions

### Does an abnormal pap smear mean I have cancer?

Rarely. Most abnormal results are caused by HPV and many resolve on their own. Colposcopy helps determine whether any treatment is needed.

### Will a LEEP affect future pregnancies?

LEEP can slightly increase the risk of preterm birth in a future pregnancy. If pregnancy is in your future plans, this is discussed with you as part of deciding on treatment.
